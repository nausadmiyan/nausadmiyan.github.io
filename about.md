I am a PhD researcher in Civil and Environmental Engineering at the University of Rhode Island, conducting interdisciplinary research on sustainable and intelligent construction materials. My academic and professional work focuses on the development of next-generation low-carbon cementitious systems that merge experimental science, numerical modeling, and data-driven frameworks. My research direction stems from a deep interest in connecting the physical mechanisms of cementitious microstructures with predictive modeling techniques, enabling the design of materials that are not only durable and efficient but also environmentally responsible.  

My current studies involve integrating laboratory experimentation with finite element modeling and machine learning to predict and optimize the performance of alkali-activated and auxetic composites. Through this work, I aim to uncover mechanistic insights into hydration reactions, microstructural evolution, and phase transitions that govern the strength, durability, and sustainability of modern binders. The utilization of industrial by-products such as blast furnace, basic oxygen furnace, and ferrochrome slags forms the foundation of my experimental studies. These materials, typically treated as waste, are transformed into high-performance binders through alkali activation, allowing substantial reductions in the carbon footprint compared to conventional Portland cement systems. I also study the coupling of these binders with phase change materials and auxetic geometries to enhance thermal stability, self-defrosting capability, and energy absorption under complex stress conditions.  

In parallel, I explore data-driven modeling and interpretability in materials design by employing techniques such as random forests, gradient boosting, neural networks, and SHAP analysis to reveal the governing compositional and curing factors affecting mechanical behavior. I have proposed a hybrid framework that combines missing data imputation, generative adversarial networks for data augmentation, and interpretable machine learning to predict the compressive strength of alkali-activated concretes with high accuracy. The integration of SHAP visualization has further enabled transparent interpretation of model predictions, identifying dominant variables such as water content, SiO₂ ratio, and curing temperature. These efforts contribute to a growing field of interpretable artificial intelligence in material informatics, advancing the reliability of predictive models in construction engineering.  

My research portfolio includes collaborative publications in high-impact journals such as Journal of Cleaner Production, Construction and Building Materials, and Journal of Building Engineering. These works collectively address the challenges of waste valorization, binder optimization, microstructural assessment, and the application of statistical and computational tools to sustainable materials. The studies have demonstrated that incorporating ferrochrome or steel slags can enhance rheology, extend setting times, and stabilize long-term microstructures without compromising mechanical strength. Other investigations have confirmed that limestone calcined clay cements integrated with microencapsulated phase change materials can mitigate freeze–thaw damage and delay ice formation on pavement surfaces, contributing to safer and longer-lasting infrastructure in cold regions.  

The overarching objective of my research is to establish a unified circular framework for material design that bridges experiments, simulations, and data interpretation. By coupling multiscale modeling with machine learning and physical experimentation, I seek to provide a foundation for designing cementitious materials that are resilient, resource-efficient, and adaptable to evolving environmental demands. My long-term vision is to leverage computational tools and generative modeling to accelerate discovery in low-carbon construction materials and to contribute toward the global transition to carbon-neutral infrastructure.
